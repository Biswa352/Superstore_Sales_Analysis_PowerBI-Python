# Superstore Sales Analysis — Case Study

## 1. Overview

I used the Sample Superstore dataset to explore sales performance across regions, product categories, and months. I built an interactive report in Power BI and added a Python visual with Matplotlib to compare average sales per order line by category.

The goal was to identify where sales were strongest, which categories contributed most to total sales, and how sales changed over time.

## 2. Business Questions

- Which regions generate the highest sales?
- Which product categories contribute most to total sales?
- How does sales performance vary by month?
- How does average sales per order line differ across categories?

## 3. Dashboard Findings

### Regional Performance

The West region leads with approximately **$0.73 million** in sales, followed by the East at approximately **$0.68 million**. Central contributes approximately **$0.50 million**, while the South records approximately **$0.39 million**.

The report shows a clear difference in sales contribution across regions, with West and East accounting for the largest portions of the displayed regional totals.

### Category Performance

Technology generates approximately **$836.15K** in sales, making it the leading category by total sales. Furniture contributes approximately **$742K**, while Office Supplies contributes approximately **$719.05K**.

Technology therefore has the largest total sales contribution in the report. The category totals show overall sales volume, but they do not by themselves explain profitability, demand, or the reasons behind the differences.

### Monthly Sales Trend

Sales vary across the year rather than remaining consistent from month to month. November is the strongest month in the displayed results, at approximately **$352.46K**, followed by December at approximately **$330K**. February is the weakest month, at approximately **$59.75K**.

The visible peaks toward the end of the year suggest that this period is important to investigate further. The dashboard alone does not establish why these peaks occur.

## 4. Python Visual: Average Sales per Order Line

I added a Python visual to compare the mean `Sales` value for each product category. The visual uses Matplotlib and groups the records by `Category`.

```python
import matplotlib.pyplot as plt

category_summary = (
    dataset.groupby("Category", as_index=False)
    ["Sales"].mean()
)

plt.figure(figsize=(8, 5))
plt.bar(
    category_summary["Category"],
    category_summary["Sales"]
)

plt.title("Average Sales per Order Line by Category")
plt.xlabel("Category")
plt.ylabel("Average Sales per Order Line")
plt.xticks(rotation=15)
plt.tight_layout()
plt.show()
```

In this visual, Technology has the highest average sales per order line, followed by Furniture and Office Supplies.

This is a different measure from total sales. The category-total chart adds sales across records, whereas this Python visual calculates the average `Sales` value per row. The two visuals should therefore be interpreted as complementary rather than interchangeable.

`Row ID` is included among the Python visual's input fields to help preserve the row-level records in the dataset passed to the script by Power BI.
